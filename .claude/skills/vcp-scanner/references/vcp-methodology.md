# VCP Methodology for NSE Markets

## Core Definition
A Volatility Contraction Pattern (VCP) is a series of price contractions where each pullback becomes progressively shallower, indicating accumulation before an upside breakout. The pattern typically forms over 3-12 weeks with 2-5 distinct contractions.

## Minervini Trend Template

### Primary Criteria (All Must Be Met)
1. **Price Above 150 DMA**: Stock must trade above its 150-day moving average
2. **Price Above 200 DMA**: Stock must trade above its 200-day moving average
3. **200 DMA Rising**: The 200-day moving average must be trending upward for at least 1 month
4. **52-Week Position**: Stock within 25% of its 52-week high and at least 30% above 52-week low
5. **Relative Strength >70**: Stock outperforming 70% of Nifty 500 stocks

### Why These Criteria Matter
- **Moving Averages**: Confirm underlying uptrend and institutional accumulation
- **52-Week Position**: Ensures stock is in strong intermediate-term position
- **Relative Strength**: Identifies leadership and momentum vs market

## Contraction Rules

### Basic Structure
- **Minimum Contractions**: 2 (acceptable but lower quality)
- **Optimal Range**: 3-5 contractions
- **Duration**: 3-12 weeks total formation time
- **Shape**: Ascending triangle or wedge pattern

### Pullback Depth Requirements
- **First Pullback**: 10-35% from swing high to swing low
- **Second Pullback**: 40-60% shallower than first (at least 30% reduction)
- **Third+ Pullbacks**: Each progressively shallower by similar magnitude
- **Final Contraction**: Often <7% range (highest quality)

### Example Contraction Sequence
```
Swing High 1: ₹1000
Pullback 1: ₹650 (35% decline) → 35% depth

Swing High 2: ₹850
Pullback 2: ₹680 (20% decline from high 2) → 43% shallower

Swing High 3: ₹780
Pullback 3: ₹740 (5% decline from high 3) → 75% shallower
```

## Volume Profile Analysis

### Base Formation Volume
- **Declining Trend**: Volume should decrease through the base formation
- **Supply Exhaustion**: Lower volume indicates reduced selling pressure
- **Final Dry-Up**: Last contraction should show <50% of 50-day average volume
- **Breakout Volume**: Must exceed 150% of 50-day average on breakout

### Volume Interpretation
- **High Volume in Base**: Indicates distribution, avoid these patterns
- **Increasing Volume**: Suggests accumulation, positive signal
- **Volume Spikes**: Can occur at swing highs (institutional buying) or lows (capitulation)

## Fundamental Filter (Indian Market Addition)

### EPS Growth Validation
- **Quarterly Comparison**: Last 2+ quarters must show positive QoQ EPS growth
- **Consistency**: Avoid one-time EPS spikes from extraordinary items
- **Trend**: Look for accelerating growth pattern
- **Margin Check**: Ensure profit margins stable or expanding

### Revenue/Sales Growth
- **YoY Growth**: Positive year-over-year revenue growth
- **Quarterly Trend**: Consistent or improving quarterly revenues
- **Segment Analysis**: Check if growth is broad-based across business segments
- **Currency Impact**: Adjust for forex fluctuations in export-oriented companies

### Why Fundamentals Matter
- **Accumulation vs Dead Money**: Technical VCP can form in dead money (no growth)
- **Sustainability**: Growth validates the breakout potential
- **Risk Reduction**: Avoid value traps masquerading as VCPs

## Pivot and Breakout Mechanics

### Pivot Identification
- **Definition**: Highest point of the final tight contraction
- **Significance**: Key resistance level that must be broken
- **Volume Requirement**: Breakout must occur on >150% of 50-day average volume
- **Confirmation**: Clean break above pivot without immediate rejection

### Breakout Entry Rules
- **Entry Trigger**: Close above pivot level
- **Volume Confirmation**: Volume surge on breakout day
- **Time Frame**: Enter on breakout day or next day open
- **Position Sizing**: Scale based on VCP quality score

## Risk Management

### Stop Loss Placement
- **Standard SL**: 5-8% below low of final contraction
- **Alternative SL**: 10-15% below breakout price
- **Trailing Stop**: Implement after 20-30% profit achieved
- **Time Stop**: Exit if no breakout within 3 weeks of pivot identification

### Position Sizing Guidelines
- **A+ Grade (80-100)**: Full position size (1-2% of portfolio)
- **A Grade (65-80)**: 75% position size
- **B Grade (50-65)**: 50% position size
- **C Grade (<50)**: Skip the setup

## Pattern Variations

### Classic VCP
- **Shape**: Symmetrical triangle with upward bias
- **Duration**: 6-10 weeks
- **Contractions**: 3-4 with clear shallowing
- **Success Rate**: 70-80% in bull markets

### Tight VCP
- **Shape**: Very narrow final contraction (<5% range)
- **Duration**: 8-12 weeks
- **Volume**: Extreme dry-up in final phase
- **Success Rate**: 80-90% when all criteria met

### Extended VCP
- **Shape**: Longer base formation (3-6 months)
- **Contractions**: 4-6 with gradual tightening
- **Volume**: Prolonged declining pattern
- **Success Rate**: 60-75% (higher reward potential)

## Common Pattern Mistakes

### False VCPs to Avoid
- **Descending Triangles**: Each contraction deeper than previous
- **Wide-Ranging Bases**: >15% range (too loose, indicates indecision)
- **High Volume Bases**: Distribution patterns, not accumulation
- **Near Earnings**: Stocks about to report results

### Technical Pitfalls
- **Wrong Pivot**: Using wrong swing high as breakout level
- **Early Entry**: Entering before confirmed breakout
- **Volume Ignored**: Breakouts without required volume surge
- **Trend Violation**: Stocks not meeting Minervini criteria

- **Average Move**: 25-40% from breakout to peak
- **Holding Period**: 4-8 weeks for full move

### Neutral Market Performance
- **Pattern Count**: 5-10 VCPs per month
- **Breakout Success**: 50-65%

- **Pattern Count**: 0-2 VCPs per month (rare)

- **Strategy**: Defensive positioning, reduced exposure
