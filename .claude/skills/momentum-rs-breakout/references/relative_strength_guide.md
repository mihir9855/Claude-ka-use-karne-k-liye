# Relative Strength Analysis Guide

## What is Relative Strength?
Relative Strength (RS) measures how a stock performs compared to the overall market or a benchmark index. It's a momentum indicator that helps identify stocks that are outperforming or underperforming the market.

## RS Ratio Calculation
```
RS Ratio = Stock Price / Benchmark Price
```

## RS Line Interpretation
- **Rising RS Line**: Stock outperforming the market
- **Falling RS Line**: Stock underperforming the market
- **Flat RS Line**: Stock moving in line with market

## RS Momentum
The rate of change in the RS ratio over time:
```
RS Momentum = (Current RS - RS from N periods ago) / RS from N periods ago
```

## Practical Applications
1. **Stock Selection**: Choose stocks with rising RS lines
2. **Sector Rotation**: Identify strong vs weak sectors
3. **Market Timing**: Use RS to time entries and exits
4. **Portfolio Construction**: Weight towards high RS stocks

## Common Timeframes
- Short-term: 3-6 months
- Medium-term: 6-12 months
- Long-term: 1-2 years

## Combining with Other Indicators
- Use with volume for confirmation
- Combine with trend indicators
- Validate with fundamental analysis

## Risk Management
- Don't chase extreme RS readings
- Monitor for RS divergence
- Use stop losses based on RS levels
- Rebalance when RS changes significantly

## Tools for RS Analysis
- Technical analysis software (TradingView, MetaStock)
- Excel spreadsheets
- Custom Python scripts
- Financial data providers (NSE, Yahoo Finance)

## Case Study: ITC vs Nifty
During 2023 bull run:
- ITC RS Ratio: 1.25 (25% outperformance)
- Consistent rising RS line
- Higher volume during upmoves
- Result: 45% returns vs Nifty's 15%
